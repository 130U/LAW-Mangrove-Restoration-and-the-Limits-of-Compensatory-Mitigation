# Where this Article sits

Conservation science has stated the conditions under which restoration can offset a loss: the values at stake
must be measurable, the capacity to restore them must rest on evidence, and time lags and uncertainty must be
accounted for. American wetland regulation built its instruments around the same concerns after the National
Research Council found the mitigation program falling short of no net loss of wetland function. Neither body of
work says where, in the instruments of a jurisdiction that already recognizes these concerns, the legal
consequence of an ecological prediction should attach. The Article answers that question for Florida and the
Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area. This page records what each body of authority settles, what it leaves
open, and what the Article adds. The complete list of 163 authorities and sources is in the
[bibliography](bibliography.md).

| **Binding law, Hong Kong** | Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance and its Technical Memorandum | Permits for designated projects; a preference for mitigation before the works | How a predicted enhancement is credited against a present loss |
| **Policy, Hong Kong and mainland** | Town Planning Board guidelines for Deep Bay; the State Council's 2016 wetland plan | No net loss in area and in function; "compensate first, occupy later" | Binding force over a particular assessment |
| **Binding law, United States** | Clean Water Act § 404; 33 C.F.R. pt. 332; *Sackett v. EPA* | The mitigation sequence, advance or concurrent compensation, ratios for temporal loss and risk, release of credits against performance | Waters outside federal jurisdiction |
| **Binding law, Florida** | Fla. Stat. § 373.414(18); Fla. Admin. Code r. 62-345.600; the Mangrove Trimming and Preservation Act | A numerical discount for time lag and risk; full credit only when the replacement precedes the loss | Function beyond canopy under the trimming statute |
| **Science and practice** | Restoration ecology; studies of Deep Bay; evaluations of mitigation | What restoration achieves, how long it takes, and how often commitments are monitored | The legal consequence of those findings |

## What the Article adds

| Body of work | Where it stops | The Article's step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offset science: Maron et al.; Bull et al.; the BBOP principles | The conditions under which an offset is credible | A legal reading of those conditions: restoration as repair and restoration as currency, with a compensation credit treated as a credit |
| United States mitigation law and its evaluations | Instruments for timing, risk and assurance | Their arithmetic applied to a project in another legal system, as a measure of what a promise is worth |
| Mangrove restoration science: Lewis; Ellison et al. | How to restore tidal conditions and how to learn from failure | The separation of engineering completion from ecological acceptance as distinct legal milestones |
| Studies of Deep Bay: Sung et al.; Choi et al. | More mangrove can mean less mudflat | The whole-habitat account as a condition of crediting |
| The law of each jurisdiction compared | A timing norm stated in each | The decision those norms should govern |

## Key authorities

### Offsets and what they can promise

<a id="maron2012"></a>
**Martine Maron et al., *Faustian Bargains? Restoration Realities in the Context of Biodiversity Offset Policies*, 155 Biological Conservation 141 (2012).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2012.06.003)
Offset policy expects more of restoration than the evidence supports. Time lags, uncertainty and measurability
confine credible restoration offsets to a small domain. The paper states the conditions in ecological terms. The
Article gives them a legal form and a place in the sequence of decisions.

<a id="bull2013"></a>
**Joseph W. Bull et al., *Biodiversity Offsets in Theory and Practice*, 47 Oryx 369 (2013).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1017/S003060531200172X)
Losses from development are certain while gains from offsets may come late or never, and the remedies are to
offset before the impact or to discount. Both remedies exist in Florida's rule. The Article shows what the second
yields on a published timetable.

<a id="bekessy2010"></a>
**Sarah A. Bekessy et al., *The Biodiversity Bank Cannot Be a Lending Bank*, 3 Conservation Letters 151 (2010).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1755-263X.2010.00110.x)
Trading a present loss for promised restoration is lending against an uncertain future, and accrued value should be
shown before it offsets a loss. The Article's account of restoration as currency takes the financial reading
further: discount, security and a schedule of draws each have a regulatory counterpart.

<a id="nrc2001"></a>
**National Research Council, Compensating for Wetland Losses Under the Clean Water Act (2001).** [text](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/10134/compensating-for-wetland-losses-under-the-clean-water-act)
The mitigation program was not meeting the goal of no net loss of wetland function. The report produced the
instruments of the 2008 federal rule. It is an evaluation of one national program and does not ask how its lessons
apply where the law already states the principle and leaves its trigger unspecified.

<a id="gao2005"></a>
**U.S. Government Accountability Office, Wetlands Protection: Corps of Engineers Does Not Have an Effective Oversight Approach to Ensure That Compensatory Mitigation Is Occurring (2005).** [report](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-05-898)
Monitoring reports and inspections were missing from much of the sample of permit files. A condition that nobody
reviews is not a condition. The Article's schedule names a reviewer, a resource and a consequence for each
commitment.

### United States and Florida law

<a id="compmit2008"></a>
**Compensatory Mitigation for Losses of Aquatic Resources, 73 Fed. Reg. 19,594 (Apr. 10, 2008) (codified at 33 C.F.R. pt. 332).** [text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2008-04-10/html/E8-6918.htm)
Compensation in advance of or concurrent with the impact, additional compensation for temporal losses, ratios for
the likelihood of success, financial assurances, and release of bank credits against performance milestones. These
are the four properties of a credit, written as regulation.

<a id="umam"></a>
**Fla. Stat. § 373.414(18); Fla. Admin. Code r. 62-345.600.** [rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-62-345-600)
The Legislature required a uniform method that accounts for the expected time lag and the degree of risk. The rule
divides the expected functional gain by a time-lag factor and a risk score, and recognizes no time lag only when
mitigation offsets the impact at or before the time of impact. On the question the Article asks, Florida has an
answer written in numbers.

<a id="mangroveact"></a>
**Mangrove Trimming and Preservation Act, Fla. Stat. §§ 403.9321-403.9333.**
Trimming exemptions with conditions, and restoration standards stated in canopy and survival. The statute is
specialized and its measure is canopy, which shows why a comparison of legal systems looks at functions and not at
the number of mangrove statutes.

<a id="sackett2023"></a>
**Sackett v. EPA, 598 U.S. 651 (2023).** [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-454_4g15.pdf)
Adjacent wetlands are covered by the Clean Water Act only with a continuous surface connection to a covered water.
Federal jurisdiction does not follow ecological importance, which bounds every lesson drawn from federal law.

### Mainland China, Hong Kong and San Tin

<a id="wpl2021"></a>
**Wetland Protection Law of the People's Republic of China (2021, amended 2026).**
The Law addresses mangroves and hydrological disruption expressly and requires replacement of equivalent area and
quality for occupied wetland. A replacement that does not yet exist cannot be shown to be equivalent, which is the
reading the Article gives article 21.

<a id="sc2016"></a>
**General Office of the State Council, Wetland Protection and Restoration System Plan, Guo Ban Fa [2016] No. 89.** [text](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2016-12/12/content_5146928.htm)
A land user whose approved project occupied wetland was to restore or rebuild wetland of equivalent area and
quality under the principle of compensating first and occupying later. The plan is no longer in force. It shows
that the order of loss and replacement was a stated element of national wetland policy before the statute.

<a id="tpb12c"></a>
**Town Planning Board, Guidelines for Application for Developments within Deep Bay Area, TPB PG-No. 12C.** [pdf](https://www.tpb.gov.hk/en/forms/Guidelines/pg12c_e.pdf)
The Board adopts the principle of no net loss in wetland, where loss refers to both area and function, measured by
what the fishponds supply to ardeids and other species. A functional standard is settled Hong Kong policy for these
wetlands.

<a id="hkbws2024"></a>
**Hong Kong Bird Watching Society, Comments on the EIA Report for San Tin/Lok Ma Chau Development Node (Mar. 2, 2024).** [pdf](https://cms.hkbws.org.hk/cms/phocadownload/submissions/EIA/20240302_STLMC_EIA_HKBWS.pdf)
The predicted 45 percent enhancement was taken from the approved 2008 assessment for Fung Lok Wai, whose
compensation measures were not implemented, and the conservation park is scheduled for completion by 2039. The
submission is a party's objection and not an adjudicated finding. It supplies the two particulars to which the
Article applies Florida's method.

### Restoration evidence

<a id="lewis2005"></a>
**Roy R. Lewis III, *Ecological Engineering for Successful Management and Restoration of Mangrove Forests*, 24 Ecological Engineering 403 (2005).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoleng.2004.10.003)
At West Lake, grades were matched to a reference forest, tidal creeks were added and all three mangrove species
recruited without planting. Restoring tidal conditions is the credible intervention where tidal restriction limits
recovery. The project was mitigation for development elsewhere, so its success as repair does not settle its
adequacy as compensation.

<a id="sung2021"></a>
**Yik-Hei Sung et al., *Ecological Correlates of 20-Year Population Trends of Wintering Waterbirds in Deep Bay, South China*, 9 Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution, art. 658084 (2021).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2021.658084)
In Deep Bay mangrove cover rose from 8 to 15 percent and other vegetation from 3 to 14 percent while mudflat fell
from 56 to 43 percent, over a period in which several waterbird species declined. The study does not attribute the
declines to mangrove expansion. It shows why additional canopy cannot stand in for the whole wetland mosaic.

<a id="choi2022"></a>
**Chi-Yeung Choi et al., *An Emerging Coastal Wetland Management Dilemma Between Mangrove Expansion and Shorebird Conservation*, 36 Conservation Biology, art. e13905 (2022).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.13905)
At shorebird sites along the Chinese coast mangrove area increased while tidal flats declined. The receiving
ecosystem contains competing conservation objectives, so a restoration objective has to be justified locally.

<a id="ellison2020"></a>
**Aaron M. Ellison, Alexander J. Felson & Daniel A. Friess, *Mangrove Rehabilitation and Restoration as Experimental Adaptive Management*, 7 Frontiers in Marine Science, art. 327 (2020).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00327)
Projects should be designed as experiments, with reference baselines, indicators and decision triggers set in
advance. That is the scientific form of the Article's rule that a plausible range is justified before work begins
and that a failed milestone has a named reviewer.

<a id="morenomateos2012"></a>
**David Moreno-Mateos et al., *Structural and Functional Loss in Restored Wetland Ecosystems*, 10 PLoS Biology, art. e1001247 (2012).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001247)
Restored wetlands remain below reference conditions in structure and function for decades. Time lag is the
ordinary case, which is why a rule that prices it matters.
